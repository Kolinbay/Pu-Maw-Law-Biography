Biography of Pu Maw Law.

က့ၤဖးလၢကညီကျိၥ်

By Researcher: Saw Mu Lar Htoo

This biography presents a concise account of Pu Maw Law's life and contributions. The researcher respectfully acknowledges that some cultural and historical elements discussed may be unfamiliar to readers. This work is presented with the sincere hope that readers will find it informative and that no distress or misunderstanding arises from its content.

Pu Maw Law was born in 1937 as the youngest of six children. His birth name was Saw Kay Poe, but people called him Pu Maw Law ("Grandpa Maw Law") after his village. He was a Karen ancestor who lived as a rural farmer near the border of Bridge 5 and Bridge 3 in Burma (Myanmar). From childhood, Pu Maw Law possessed special talents as an educated Shaman. He received this gift through spiritual dreams, as was common in Animist traditions of Southeast Asia. This remarkable ability was partly inherited from his ancestors and became his lifelong passion. Known for his honesty and kindness, he used his healing powers to save many lives and became famous throughout the region. During a period when rats destroyed crops and caused widespread hunger in his area, many villagers were forced to relocate. However, Pu Maw Law used his predictive abilities to determine it was safe for his family to remain in their village.

In 1997, Myanmar's military dictatorship (specifically Unit-77) began attacking rural areas, killing residents and forcing many to flee into the jungle. The military established camps on Ka Baw Tu, Kyar Gaw Kyone, and Ter Ner Kyone mountains, making farming extremely difficult. Pu Maw Law escaped to Yeh Mu Plaw village at Bridge 5, where he continued healing people and gained greater recognition. After several years, he moved to Du Daw Khee village in Bridge 3, though military presence remained a threat. The military established another camp on Bu Tho Kho mountain to oversee a dam project on the Khay Lo Kloe river, creating additional hardships for local residents. During this difficult time, Pu Maw Law became crucial to the community by predicting military movements, as the Karen resistance lacked modern communication systems. Even Karen General Saw Pa Thu Pi relied on Pu Maw Law's guidance for safety. Pu Maw Law often sacrificed sleep to treat patients and care for relatives and neighbors in need. After living in Pa Ku Hser Der for several years, he returned to his original village when military activities decreased.

Between 2012-2013, Pu Maw Law returned to farming in his ancestral lands, protected by his shamanic powers. In 2014, he resettled in Khaw Wah Hta (Maw Law village) and invited his children, grandchildren, and other relatives to return. Throughout his life, people sought his healing abilities. After suffering from various illnesses and the effects of aging, he passed away in late 2015. His village held a seven-day funeral that drew many mourners, including those with disabilities who had benefited from his care. By 2015-2016, his children and villagers had completely resettled Maw Law Village.

Pu Maw Law's most treasured saying was "Patience overcomes everything," a philosophy his followers continue to value. Before his death, he revealed to his son that he had suffered from harmful magic created by a jealous man named Saw Than Shwe Htoo, who targeted successful healers out of envy. According to Karen wisdom, "You do it and you get it" - meaning negative actions bring negative consequences. Eventually, Saw Than Shwe Htoo was rejected by his family and community, forcing him to leave the village permanently.
